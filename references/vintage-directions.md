# Vintage Directions

Choose one primary direction per card. Combine no more than two directions, and only when the scene benefits from it.

## 1900s hand-tinted travel card

- Use a faded photographic plate with translucent hand-applied color that slightly misses contours.
- Favor ivory, dusty rose, pale cyan, olive, tobacco brown, and softened black.
- Show silvering near dark edges, gentle emulsion scratches, uneven exposure, and rubbed corners.
- Use narrow serif or small caps typography with sparse ornamental rules.
- Best for portraits, wildlife, architecture, and quiet landscapes.

## 1930s lithographic tourism poster

- Preserve the subject faithfully inside a simplified lithographic rendering with broad contour-led masses.
- Favor mustard, oxidized teal, faded vermilion, warm cream, ultramarine, and soot black.
- Show paper tooth, ink starvation, plate wear, slight color misregistration, and sun-faded margins.
- Use condensed display type, geometric borders, route lines, or registration marks.
- Best for hiking, mountains, coastlines, transport, and strong silhouettes.

## 1950s offset picture postcard

- Use a full-bleed or inset photograph with coarse CMYK rosette, softened contrast, cyan-red color drift, and selectively retained detail.
- Favor faded cyan, warm red, yellowed cream, deep blue, and gray-black.
- Show uneven varnish loss, corner rub, fine scratches, small foxing blooms, and a slightly darkened edge.
- Use friendly grotesk or script accents with a compact postal block.
- Best for travel snapshots, groups, animals, ice, water, and city scenes.

## 1960s screenprint field card

- Combine a recognizable photographic anchor with two or three flat screenprinted fields, rough stencil edges, and deliberate ink overlap.
- Favor one muted base pair plus one brighter source-derived accent; keep total inks between two and four.
- Show mesh texture, pinholes, dry pulls, imperfect registration, and localized abrasion.
- Use bold condensed type or small modular labels; move postal marks to support the composition.
- Best for graphic landscapes, foliage, snow, uniforms, and scenes with directional movement.

## Variety routing

- For a single card, select the direction from subject, light, native color, and emotional temperature—not randomly.
- For batches of four or more, use at least two directions unless the user asks for strict uniformity.
- Keep one family constant, such as paper era, border tone, or typography, while changing the print method and layout.
- Never let every image default to cream paper, tomato orange, upper-right stamp, centered tear, and identical micro-text placement.
